# What Is Cowork?

This is the module where people go *"oh, this changes how I work."* Cowork is Claude that stops *answering* and starts *doing*.

## Chat answers; Cowork delivers

Anthropic noticed something: non-technical teams (marketing, data, ops) were reaching for **Claude Code** — the developer tool — because it could take on whole multi-step jobs, not just respond one message at a time. **Cowork** brings that same agentic capability to everyone, wrapped in a friendly interface **right inside Claude Desktop — no terminal required.**

:::concept The mindset shift: outcome, not prompt
- **Chat** is built around the **prompt**: you ask, it answers, you ask again. You coordinate every step.
- **Cowork** is built around the **outcome**: you describe the *finished result you want*, and Claude plans the steps, does the work on your files, and hands you a deliverable.

> "Most AI tools are built around the prompt. Cowork is built around the outcome."
:::

So instead of: *ask → copy → paste → ask → copy → paste…*, you say one thing like:

> "Read these 12 vendor PDFs in my Downloads, pull pricing and contract length, and give me a one-page comparison table plus a recommendation."

…then step away and come back to a finished comparison.

## What makes Cowork different

- **It works on your real files.** Cowork can read from and write to folders on your computer that you choose — no manual upload/download dance.
- **It produces real deliverables.** Not just text in a chat: Excel spreadsheets with working formulas, PowerPoint decks, formatted Word docs, organized folders.
- **It divides and conquers.** For big jobs it coordinates **subagents** working in parallel, so a 10-step task doesn't crawl.
- **It can run on a schedule.** Save a task and have it run automatically on a cadence (e.g., every Monday 8am) — something a normal chat can't do.
- **It remembers, via Projects.** Group related tasks into a persistent workspace with their own files, instructions, and memory.
- **It can use the web.** Paired with **Claude in Chrome**, it can navigate sites, click, and fill forms to get things done online.

## When to reach for Cowork

:::tip The Cowork test
Reach for Cowork when a task is **high-effort, multi-step, repeatable, and touches files** — and you'd happily walk away and check the result later.

✅ Great fits:
- "Organize this chaotic folder and summarize what's in it."
- "Turn these 5 interview transcripts into a themed insights report."
- "Build a spreadsheet that tracks these tickets with status and owner."
- "Every Friday, compile what shipped this week into a draft update."

❌ Not Cowork (just chat):
- "What's a good headline for this?"
- "Explain this concept."
- "Reword this paragraph."
:::

## The honest part: access & trust

Because Cowork can **read and change real files** on your computer, it needs more trust than chat. You stay in control:

- **You choose the folder(s)** Cowork can access — it works in that designated space, not your whole machine.
- Code and shell commands run in an **isolated** way — but **computer use** (when Cowork controls your browser or desktop apps) is **not** sandboxed, and either way **it can make real changes to the files you share.**
- Start it on a **copy** of important files while you're learning (we'll do exactly that in the lab).

:::warning Requirements
Cowork needs a **paid plan** (Pro, Max, Team, or Enterprise) and the **Claude Desktop app**. If you don't see a **Cowork** entry in your sidebar, check your plan and that the app is up to date. You can still read these lessons and do the lab later.

Cowork is currently a **research preview**, so screens and features may shift — if yours looks a little different from these lessons, that's expected.
:::
